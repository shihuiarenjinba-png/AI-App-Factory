# Market research — Double-Entry Bookkeeping Desk

Date: 2026-10-31

## Positioning

This product is a conventional local double-entry ledger. It is deliberately smaller than full accounting suites and avoids cloud accounts, subscriptions, bank credentials and automatic tax claims.

## Official basis

IRS recordkeeping guidance describes journals and ledgers as ordinary summaries of business transactions and allows systems suited to the business that clearly show income and expenses:
- https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/recordkeeping
- https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-should-i-record-my-business-transactions

Japan's National Tax Agency guidance describes journals, general ledgers and other books as recognized bookkeeping records:
- https://www.nta.go.jp/publication/pamph/01.htm
- https://www.keisan.nta.go.jp/r3yokuaru/aoiroshinkoku/aoiroshinkokuseido/aoiroshinkokuseido/seido1.html

## Competitors

- GnuCash: free open-source desktop double-entry accounting. Strongest free alternative.
  https://www.gnucash.org/
- Doric: local-first Windows accounting with no account/subscription, also a direct local-first competitor.
  https://doricapp.com/
- QuickBooks: strong cloud bookkeeping ecosystem.
  https://quickbooks.intuit.com/
- FreshBooks: subscription accounting/invoicing for freelancers and small businesses.
  https://www.freshbooks.com/pricing
- Yayoi installed accounting products in Japan are a strong desktop competitor.
  https://www.yayoi-kk.co.jp/products/price/

## Differentiation hypothesis

A narrowly scoped Windows app may appeal to people who want a bilingual, understandable journal/trial-balance workflow and local ownership without learning the broader feature surface of GnuCash or buying a cloud subscription.

The market is crowded and has strong free/local competitors. No MARKET_FIT_PASS is claimed without direct user evidence.
